An exact Lindblad propagator is completely positive and trace preserving (CPTP); a direct Runge–Kutta (RK) step applied to the same generator need not be. For the autonomous phase-covariant qubit generator (coherent precession, bidirectional relaxation, pure dephasing) we derive necessary and sufficient conditions for a direct RK step to be CPTP, expressed through the stability function evaluated at the generator's eigenvalues. For one-way relaxation without dephasing these conditions classify classical RK4 into six admissibility regimes as the precession-to-relaxation ratio varies, including regimes where the admissible step set is disconnected or reduces to isolated points. Exactly removing the commuting precession in a rotating frame eliminates the high-frequency disconnected component, so finite-step physicality depends on the numerical representation, not only on the method. Pointwise certificates at four transverse-field strengths show the disconnected structure persists under a noncommuting perturbation. Candidate construction matters independently: at precession ratio 2 the positive-step CPTP sets of one full RK4 step and of two half steps are disjoint, and the fifth-order Richardson extrapolate of RK4 is non-CPTP on the entire step range where both constituents are CPTP. We implement a tri-state guard that certifies the executed binary64 step and candidate before acceptance and fails closed on unresolved decisions. In adaptive tests, error-only step doubling accepts non-CPTP candidates; the guarded workflows project to a certified step or fall back to an exact subflow. The executed candidate, not the method label or the error estimate, determines finite-step physicality.
