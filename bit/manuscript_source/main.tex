\abstract{For the autonomous phase-covariant qubit generator with coherent precession, upward and downward relaxation, and pure dephasing, we give a necessary and sufficient finite-step complete-positivity criterion for Runge--Kutta methods with real coefficients. The criterion evaluates the known Choi inequalities on the stability function. On one-way, zero-dephasing rays, a signed defect law gives the leading margin coefficient for direct steps and Richardson extrapolates. For the classical fourth-order method on these rays, we classify six regimes of the admissible step set as the precession-to-relaxation ratio varies, including disconnected sets and isolated admissible steps, and certify the critical frequencies. Removing the commuting precession before discretization removes the high-frequency component. One full step and two half steps can have disjoint positive-step admissible sets. On nonrotating one-way damping without dephasing, the fifth-order Richardson extrapolate is not completely positive at any positive step for which both constituents are channels. Four pointwise certificates establish disconnectedness under a noncommuting transverse-field perturbation. An acceptance test uses exact rational arithmetic to certify the specified map at the executed binary64 step, rejecting unresolved cases. Adaptive tests show that it rejects non-channel updates accepted by error control alone.}

\keywords{Lindblad equation, complete positivity, Runge--Kutta methods, stability regions, Richardson extrapolation, certified numerics}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

A finite-dimensional Markovian open quantum system evolves under a Gorini--Kossakowski--Sudarshan--Lindblad (GKSL) generator $\Lgen$, a linear operator on the density matrices of the system, and the exact flow $\e^{t\Lgen}$ is completely positive and trace preserving (CPTP) for every $t\ge0$ \cite{Lindblad1976,GoriniKossakowskiSudarshan1976,BreuerPetruccione2002}. Complete positivity is a property of the map, not of one trajectory: a linear map $\Phi$ on $2\times2$ matrices is completely positive if and only if its Choi matrix $J(\Phi)=\sum_{jk}|j\rangle\langle k|\otimes\Phi(|j\rangle\langle k|)$ is positive semidefinite \cite{Choi1975CP,Kraus1971}. The distinction matters whenever a numerical update is reused for many inputs or applied to one subsystem of a larger register, because a map that keeps every input density matrix positive can still produce a non-positive output on an entangled input.

Applied to the linear system $\dot\rho=\Lgen\rho$, an $s$-stage Runge--Kutta (RK) method with stability function $R$ advances the state by the linear map $R(h\Lgen)$. Riesch and Jirauschek \cite{RieschJirauschek2019} showed that the RK and predictor--corrector update maps are not CPTP in general, whereas the matrix exponential is. A substantial literature constructs structure-preserving updates through Kraus-form, integrating-factor, nested-Picard, Gregory-quadrature, tensor-train, quantum-channel, and exponential Euler discretizations \cite{RieschPiklJirauschek2020,CaoLu2025,AppeloCheng2025,HuAppeloCheng2025NPI,HuAppeloCheng2026Gregory,DelMastroAppeloCheng2026TT,RobinRouchonSellem2025,ChenEtAl2026}. These constructions have different guarantees: preserving density matrices after state-dependent trace normalization is distinct from defining a linear CPTP map \cite{RobinRouchonSellem2025}. On the numerical-analysis side, positivity, contractivity, absolute monotonicity, and strong-stability preservation give step-size thresholds under which an RK method preserves a convex constraint on the solution vector \cite{BolleyCrouzeix1978,Kraaijevanger1991Contractivity,Horvath2005Threshold,GottliebShuTadmor2001}; exponential RK methods of order above two cannot preserve positivity throughout the linear problem class, including time-dependent inhomogeneity, considered in \cite{OstermannVanDaele2007}; and positivity can be enforced adaptively by perturbing the RK weights \cite{NussleinRanochaKetcheson2021}. None of these results answers the question that a practitioner running an unchanged RK code on a Lindblad equation faces: for a fixed generator and a fixed way of building the update from RK stages, which step sizes produce a quantum channel?

This paper answers that question exactly for the autonomous phase-covariant qubit generator, the family combining coherent precession about the $z$ axis, downward and upward relaxation, and pure dephasing, whose channel geometry is classical \cite{RuskaiSzarekWerner2002,Filippov2020Phase,Siudzinska2023Geometry}. Because the generator acts diagonally on a population mode and a coherence mode, the RK map is determined by two scalar values of the stability function, and the known Choi conditions for phase-covariant channels pull back to explicit inequalities in the step size. Complete positivity implies per-mode absolute stability for this same linear candidate, but the converse fails: the channel inequality couples the modes, and the admissible set can be disconnected or contain isolated points, which the threshold-type positivity and contractivity results of \cite{BolleyCrouzeix1978,Kraaijevanger1991Contractivity,Horvath2005Threshold} do not describe, since a threshold is the maximal attached interval. The amplification-matrix stability analysis of Gregory nested-Picard schemes \cite{HuAppeloCheng2026Gregory} concerns a different constructed update without trace renormalization, not the Choi admissibility of the unchanged RK map.

For every RK method with real coefficients, Theorem~\ref{thm:criterion} gives the finite-step criterion and Theorem~\ref{thm:defect} relates its local behaviour to the first defect from the exponential. On the one-way, zero-dephasing ray the leading coefficient of the complete-positivity margin is $(-1)^m\eta_mG_m(\varpi)$, where $\eta_mz^m$ is the first defect of $R(z)-\e^z$ and $G_m$ is an explicit polynomial in the precession-to-relaxation ratio $\varpi$. When this coefficient is nonzero, its sign determines small-step admissibility. Corollary~\ref{cor:extrapolation} extends the law to $n$-fold Richardson extrapolates of any method. Theorem~\ref{thm:rk4} then classifies the admissible set of the classical fourth-order method completely on that ray: six regimes as $\varpi$ varies, with certified algebraic critical frequencies, a narrow reentrant band, a regime without positive admissible steps, isolated admissible steps, and a detached high-frequency component whose edges we characterize asymptotically.

Section~\ref{sec:candidates} examines how the construction of an update changes its admissible set. Transforming to the rotating frame before discretization yields a different linear map with the zero-precession admissible set (Proposition~\ref{prop:frame}). One full step and two half steps can have disjoint positive-step admissible sets, with an exact threshold below which repeated halving skips the admissible component (Propositions~\ref{prop:composition}--\ref{prop:ladder}). On nonrotating one-way damping without dephasing, the fifth-order Richardson extrapolate of the classical method is non-CPTP at every positive step of the interval on which both constituents are channels (Theorem~\ref{thm:richardson}). Theorem~\ref{thm:persistence} establishes persistence of disconnectedness under small positive dephasing or bidirectional relaxation, and four exact certificates show two admissible components at transverse-field strengths $\Omega_x/\Gamma\in\{0,0.05,0.1,0.2\}$. Finally, an acceptance test with three outcomes certifies the executed binary64 step by exact rational arithmetic; in adaptive tests, it rejects non-channel updates accepted by error control alone.

Our earlier research note \cite{PierpointBernardLiu2026Note}, with code archived at \cite{PierpointBernardLiu2026Zenodo}, reported the zero-precession signed-defect law, certified admissible sets for eight RK formulas on zero-temperature amplitude damping (including $[0,\alpha_4]$ for the classical fourth-order method), the positive-dephasing buffer, and the composition and fixed-horizon observations. The present work develops the frequency-dependent defect law and its Richardson extension, the RK4 frequency classification, and the representation, transverse-field, and candidate-specific certification results beyond that earlier material. Exact certificates, sign charts, and derivations that are not needed to follow the proofs are collected in Online Resource~1; the accompanying reproducibility package is described in the Declarations.

\section{Phase-covariant generator and Runge--Kutta candidates}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Generator, coordinates, and modes}\label{sec:generator}

Units with $\hbar=1$ are used throughout, and decimal expansions followed by an ellipsis are truncated, not rounded. Let $|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$ be the lower- and upper-energy states, $\sigma_-=|0\rangle\langle1|$, $\sigma_+=|1\rangle\langle0|$, and $\sigma_z=|0\rangle\langle0|-|1\rangle\langle1|$. The autonomous phase-covariant generator is
\begin{equation}
 \Lgen(\rho)=-\ii[H,\rho]+\gamma_\downarrow\mathcal D[\sigma_-](\rho)+\gamma_\uparrow\mathcal D[\sigma_+](\rho)+\frac{\gamma_\phi}{2}(\sigma_z\rho\sigma_z-\rho),
 \label{eq:gksl}
\end{equation}
with $H=-\omega\sigma_z/2$, $\mathcal D[L](\rho)=L\rho L^\dagger-\{L^\dagger L,\rho\}/2$, and nonnegative rates $\gamma_\downarrow,\gamma_\uparrow,\gamma_\phi$ \cite{Filippov2020Phase,Siudzinska2023Geometry}. Let $\Gamma=\gamma_\downarrow+\gamma_\uparrow>0$ and define the dimensionless quantities
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \theta&=\frac{\gamma_\uparrow}{\Gamma}\in[0,1],&
 \kappa&=\frac{\gamma_\phi}{\Gamma}\ge0,&
 \varpi&=\frac{|\omega|}{\Gamma}\ge0,\\
 x&=\Gamma h,& u&=\gamma_\phi h=\kappa x,& \nu&=\omega h,
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:coordinates}
\end{equation}
where $h>0$ is the step size; $\theta$ is the stationary excitation fraction, and $|\nu|=\varpi x$. The generator acts on the population $\rho_{11}$ and the coherence $\rho_{01}$ by
\begin{equation}
 \dot\rho_{11}=-\Gamma(\rho_{11}-\theta),\qquad
 \dot\rho_{01}=-\Big(\frac\Gamma2+\gamma_\phi-\ii\omega\Big)\rho_{01},
 \label{eq:modes}
\end{equation}
while $\Tr\rho$ is conserved. Consequently $\spec(\Lgen)=\{0,\,-\Gamma,\,-(\Gamma/2+\gamma_\phi\mp\ii\omega)\}$, and the exact semigroup multiplies the population deviation $\rho_{11}-\theta$ by $a_{\rm ex}=\e^{-x}$ and the coherence by $c_{\rm ex}=\e^{-x/2-u+\ii\nu}$. The limit $\Gamma\to0$ is regular and does not require $\theta$.

\subsection{Direct Runge--Kutta steps}\label{sec:rkstep}

For an $s$-stage RK method with real Butcher coefficients $(A,b)$ let
\begin{equation}
 R(z)=1+z\,b^{\mathsf T}(I-zA)^{-1}\mathbf 1
 \label{eq:stability_function}
\end{equation}
be its stability function \cite{HairerNorsettWanner1993}. Applied to the linear autonomous system $\dot\rho=\Lgen\rho$, one step of size $h$ is the linear map $R(h\Lgen)$, provided the stage equations are solvable, that is $\det(I-\lambda A)\ne0$ for every $\lambda\in\spec(h\Lgen)$. We call this set of steps the \emph{stage-evaluation domain}; for explicit methods it is unrestricted, and for implicit methods it excludes the poles of $R$ reached by $-x$ and $-x/2-u\pm\ii\nu$. A removable cancellation in the reduced rational expression of $R$ does not regularize a singular stage system, so all statements below are made on the stage-evaluation domain.

All methods considered in the accuracy and order comparisons are consistent, $R'(0)=1$. The finite-step channel criterion itself does not require this consistency assumption.

Because $\Lgen$ acts diagonally on the modes \eqref{eq:modes}, the direct step is determined by two scalar values of $R$,
\begin{equation}
 a=R(-x),\qquad c=R(-x/2-u+\ii\nu),
 \label{eq:multipliers}
\end{equation}
and it acts on $X=(X_{jk})\in M_2(\mathbb C)$ as
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_\theta(a,c)(X)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 A_\theta X_{00}+C_\theta X_{11} & cX_{01}\\
 \bar cX_{10} & B_\theta X_{00}+D_\theta X_{11}
 \end{pmatrix},
 \label{eq:direct_map}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 A_\theta=1-\theta(1-a),\qquad B_\theta=\theta(1-a),\qquad
 C_\theta=(1-\theta)(1-a),\qquad D_\theta=\theta+(1-\theta)a.
 \label{eq:ABCD}
\end{equation}
Since the RK coefficients are real, $a$ is real, and $\Phi_\theta(a,c)$ preserves the trace ($A_\theta+B_\theta=C_\theta+D_\theta=1$) and Hermiticity for every step in the stage-evaluation domain. Only complete positivity is at stake. Replacing $\omega$ by $-\omega$ conjugates $c$, which changes neither $|c|$ nor any statement below; this is why only $\varpi=|\omega|/\Gamma$ enters.

\subsection{Candidate maps and representation}\label{sec:candidate_def}

An adaptive method may construct several linear maps over one interval but use only one to advance the solution. We distinguish these constructions because their complete-positivity properties can differ.

\begin{definition}[Candidate maps]\label{def:candidate}
Fix the generator \eqref{eq:gksl}, a method with stability function $R$, and a total dimensionless step $x>0$ (total dephasing step $u$, total phase $\nu$). The \emph{full-step candidate} is $\Phi_R(x)=R(h\Lgen)$, with multipliers \eqref{eq:multipliers}. The \emph{equal-substep candidate} with $n\ge2$ substeps is $\Phi_R^{(n)}(x)=\Phi_R(x/n)^n$. The \emph{extrapolated candidate} of linear order $p$ is
\begin{equation}
 E_n\Phi_R(x)=\frac{n^p\,\Phi_R^{(n)}(x)-\Phi_R(x)}{n^p-1},
 \label{eq:extrapolated_candidate}
\end{equation}
for $n=2$ and $p=4$ this is the standard Richardson extrapolate $(16\Phi_R^{(2)}-\Phi_R)/15$. The \emph{rotating-frame candidate} is defined in Section~\ref{sec:frame}. A \emph{projected candidate} is a full-step or equal-substep candidate re-evaluated at a smaller certified step, and a \emph{fallback candidate} is a map produced by a different propagator over the same interval; both are defined in Section~\ref{sec:guard}.
\end{definition}

Every candidate in Definition~\ref{def:candidate} shares the block structure \eqref{eq:direct_map} with its own multipliers: composition multiplies $a$ and $c$ (Proposition~\ref{prop:composition}), and the extrapolated candidate has multipliers $E_n R(-x)$ and $E_nR(-x/2-u+\ii\nu)$ with $E_nR(z)=\big(n^pR(z/n)^n-R(z)\big)/(n^p-1)$, because \eqref{eq:direct_map} is affine in $(a,c)$ for fixed $\theta$.

By the \emph{laboratory frame} we mean discretization of $\Lgen$ itself; by the \emph{rotating frame} we mean exact transformation of the equation to the interaction picture of $H$ followed by discretization of the transformed generator. These produce different linear maps over the same interval, and Section~\ref{sec:frame} quantifies the difference.

\subsection{Complete positivity and the admissible set}\label{sec:cp_def}

With the convention $J(\Phi)=\sum_{jk}|j\rangle\langle k|\otimes\Phi(|j\rangle\langle k|)$, the map \eqref{eq:direct_map} has the Choi matrix
\begin{equation}
 J_\theta(a,c)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 A_\theta&0&0&c\\
 0&B_\theta&0&0\\
 0&0&C_\theta&0\\
 \bar c&0&0&D_\theta
 \end{pmatrix},
 \label{eq:choi}
\end{equation}
which is the established Choi form of a non-unital phase-covariant qubit map \cite{Choi1975CP,RuskaiSzarekWerner2002,Siudzinska2023Geometry}; $\Phi$ is completely positive if and only if $J\succeq0$. For a candidate family and a fixed generator ray $(\theta,\kappa,\varpi)$ we write
\begin{equation}
 \Adm_R(\varpi)=\{x\ge0:\ \Phi_R(x)\ \text{is CPTP}\},
 \label{eq:admissible_set}
\end{equation}
and $\Adm_R^{(n)}$, $\Adm_R^{\rm ext}$ for the substep and extrapolated candidates; $\Adm_4$ denotes the set of the classical fourth-order method. The step $x=0$ always belongs to these sets. We say that a set has a \emph{positive-step component} when it contains an interval of positive length, and that a component is \emph{attached} if it contains $0$ and \emph{detached} otherwise.

\section{Complete-positivity criterion and boundary orientation}\label{sec:criterion}

\subsection{The finite-step criterion}\label{sec:finite_step}

\begin{theorem}[Finite-step criterion]\label{thm:criterion}
Let $R$ be the stability function of a Runge--Kutta method with real coefficients, let $(x,u,\nu)$ lie in the stage-evaluation domain, and let $a=R(-x)$ and $c=R(-x/2-u+\ii\nu)$. The direct step $\Phi_\theta(a,c)$ of \eqref{eq:direct_map} is trace preserving and Hermiticity preserving, and it is completely positive if and only if
\begin{equation}
 a\le1,\qquad A_\theta\ge0,\qquad D_\theta\ge0,\qquad
 \Fmargin(x,u,\nu):=a+\theta(1-\theta)(1-a)^2-|c|^2\ge0.
 \label{eq:criterion}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Trace and Hermiticity preservation were established after \eqref{eq:direct_map}. After a permutation of the basis, the Choi matrix \eqref{eq:choi} is the direct sum of the scalars $B_\theta$ and $C_\theta$ and of the Hermitian block $\big(\begin{smallmatrix}A_\theta&c\\ \bar c&D_\theta\end{smallmatrix}\big)$. For $\theta\in(0,1)$ both scalars are nonnegative exactly when $a\le1$; for $\theta=0$ only $C_\theta=1-a$ is present and for $\theta=1$ only $B_\theta=1-a$, so in every case the scalar conditions reduce to $a\le1$. The block is positive semidefinite exactly when its diagonal entries and its determinant are nonnegative, and direct multiplication gives $A_\theta D_\theta=a+\theta(1-\theta)(1-a)^2$.
\end{proof}

The criterion specializes the known Choi conditions of phase-covariant qubit channels \cite{RuskaiSzarekWerner2002,Siudzinska2023Geometry} to the stability function. We study the resulting inequalities \eqref{eq:criterion} as functions of the step size. Two features will be useful. First, at the one-way endpoints $\theta\in\{0,1\}$ the criterion reduces to
\begin{equation}
 0\le a\le1,\qquad \Mmargin(x):=a-|c|^2\ge0,
 \label{eq:one_way_criterion}
\end{equation}
where the margin already implies $a\ge0$, and the lower eigenvalue of the active block is
\begin{equation}
 \lambda_-=\frac{1+a-\sqrt{(1-a)^2+4|c|^2}}{2}
 =\frac{2\Mmargin}{1+a+\sqrt{(1-a)^2+4|c|^2}}\,,
 \label{eq:lambda_margin}
\end{equation}
so its sign is the sign of $\Mmargin$; the population ceiling $a\le1$ and the block margin are independent exit mechanisms from the channel set. Complete positivity also implies absolute stability of every mode: \eqref{eq:criterion} gives $|a|\le1$ and $|c|^2\le A_\theta D_\theta\le1$, so $\Adm_R(\varpi)\subseteq\{x:\ |R(-x)|\le1,\ |R(-x/2-u+\ii\nu)|\le1\}$, and the difference between the two sets is the subject of this article. Second, the exact semigroup has $a_{\rm ex}=\e^{-x}$ and $|c_{\rm ex}|^2=\e^{-x-2u}$, hence
\begin{equation}
 F_{{\rm ex},\theta}=\e^{-x}\big(1-\e^{-2u}\big)+\theta(1-\theta)\big(1-\e^{-x}\big)^2,
 \label{eq:exact_buffer}
\end{equation}
which, for $x>0$, vanishes exactly when $u=0$ and $\theta\in\{0,1\}$. On these one-way, zero-dephasing rays the exact flow lies on the boundary of the channel set, so the discretization error determines which side contains the numerical step. Elsewhere the exact flow lies in the relative interior, with a positive margin. For a consistent method, $R(z)=1+z+\tau_2z^2+O(z^3)$, the buffers are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \Fmargin(x,u,\nu)&=2u+O\big(x^2+xu+u^2+\nu^2\big),\\
 \Fmargin(x,0,\varpi x)&=\Big[\theta(1-\theta)+\frac{\tau_2}{2}-\frac14+(2\tau_2-1)\varpi^2\Big]x^2+O(x^3),
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:buffers}
\end{equation}
so dephasing supplies a first-order buffer and bidirectional relaxation a second-order buffer $\theta(1-\theta)x^2$ for every method of order at least two ($\tau_2=1/2$). The dephasing-buffer mechanism was already described in our research note \cite{PierpointBernardLiu2026Note}. For every consistent method any $\kappa>0$ therefore yields a positive attached admissible interval, and for a method of order at least two so does any $\theta\in(0,1)$; the lengths are quantified in Section~\ref{sec:persistence}.

\subsection{Signed first-exponential-defect law}\label{sec:defect}

We now fix the boundary rays $\theta\in\{0,1\}$, $u=0$, $\nu=\pm\varpi x$, write $\alpha=\tfrac12-\ii\varpi$, and suppose that
\begin{equation}
 R(z)-\e^z=\eta_mz^m+O(z^{m+1}),\qquad m\ge2,\qquad \eta_m\ne0,
 \label{eq:first_defect}
\end{equation}
so that $m-1$ is the linear order of the method. Define
\begin{equation}
 G_m(\varpi)=1-2\,\Re\,\alpha^m,\qquad q=1+4\varpi^2,\qquad \sigma_m=2^m(\alpha^m+\bar\alpha^m).
 \label{eq:G_definition}
\end{equation}
Because $\alpha$ and $\bar\alpha$ are the roots of $z^2-z+q/4$, the numbers $\sigma_m$ satisfy $\sigma_0=\sigma_1=2$, $\sigma_m=2\sigma_{m-1}-q\sigma_{m-2}$, and $G_m=1-\sigma_m/2^m$ is a polynomial in $q$; for instance
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 G_2&=\frac q2,& G_3&=\frac{3q}{4},& G_4&=-\frac{q(q-8)}{8},\\
 G_5&=-\frac{5q(q-4)}{16},& G_6&=\frac{q(q^2-18q+48)}{32},& G_7&=\frac{7q(q-4)^2}{64}.
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:G_factors}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[Signed first-defect law]\label{thm:defect}
Let $R$ be analytic at $0$ with real Taylor coefficients and satisfy \eqref{eq:first_defect}. Then, on the boundary rays,
\begin{equation}
 \Mmargin(x)=R(-x)-\big|R(-\alpha x)\big|^2=(-1)^m\eta_mG_m(\varpi)\,x^m+O(x^{m+1}).
 \label{eq:defect_law}
\end{equation}
Hence if $G_m(\varpi)\ne0$ there is $x_0>0$ such that for $0<x<x_0$ the direct step is CPTP when $(-1)^m\eta_mG_m(\varpi)>0$ and non-CPTP when $(-1)^m\eta_mG_m(\varpi)<0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
From \eqref{eq:first_defect}, $R(-x)=\e^{-x}+(-1)^m\eta_mx^m+O(x^{m+1})$ and $R(-\alpha x)=\e^{-\alpha x}+(-1)^m\eta_m\alpha^mx^m+O(x^{m+1})$. Since $\Re\alpha=\tfrac12$, $|\e^{-\alpha x}|^2=\e^{-x}$, and $\e^{-\bar\alpha x}=1+O(x)$, so
$|R(-\alpha x)|^2=\e^{-x}+2(-1)^m\eta_m\,\Re(\alpha^m)\,x^m+O(x^{m+1})$. Subtracting gives \eqref{eq:defect_law}; all semigroup terms of lower order cancel exactly. For small $x$ the constraint $0\le a\le1$ holds because $a=1-x+O(x^2)$, so the sign of $\Mmargin$ decides membership by \eqref{eq:one_way_criterion}.
\end{proof}

The hypothesis holds for the stability function of every RK method with real coefficients, for the compositions $R(z/n)^n$, and for the extrapolates $E_nR$ of Definition~\ref{def:candidate}, so the law applies to every candidate of Section~\ref{sec:candidate_def}. It is the channel-inequality analogue of the local sector structure of the order star of $R$ at the origin \cite{IserlesNorsett1991OrderStars}, whose $2m$ sectors are fixed by the sign of $\Re(\eta_mz^m)$: the same first defect $\eta_m$ controls both, but here it is multiplied by the frequency factor $G_m$, whose real zeros reverse the orientation. At $q=4$, that is $\varpi=\sqrt3/2$, $\alpha=\e^{-\ii\pi/3}$ and $G_m(\sqrt3/2)=1-2\cos(m\pi/3)$, which vanishes exactly when $m\equiv\pm1\pmod 6$; a zero of odd multiplicity reverses the orientation, whereas a zero of even multiplicity ($G_7$ at $q=4$) leaves it the same on both sides, and at the zero itself the sign is decided by the next coefficient. When $G_m(\varpi)=0$ the next coefficient is
\begin{equation}

\subsection{Extrapolated candidates}\label{sec:extrapolation}

Richardson extrapolation of RK steps is classical, and its effect on absolute stability, dissipation, and dispersion has been studied in detail \cite{Szyszkowicz1985Richardson,HavasiKazemi2018Richardson}. Theorem~\ref{thm:defect} yields its effect on complete positivity for every method.

\begin{corollary}[Extrapolated candidates]\label{cor:extrapolation}
Let $R$ have real coefficients and linear order $p\ge1$, $R(z)-\e^z=\eta_{p+1}z^{p+1}+\eta_{p+2}z^{p+2}+O(z^{p+3})$, and let $n\ge2$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item $E_nR(z)-\e^z=\eta^E_{p+2}z^{p+2}+O(z^{p+3})$ with
 \begin{equation}
  \eta^E_{p+2}=\frac{(n-1)(\eta_{p+1}-\eta_{p+2})}{n\,(n^p-1)};
  \label{eq:extrapolation_defect}
 \end{equation}
 in particular the extrapolated candidate has linear order $p+1$, and a higher order exactly when $\eta_{p+1}=\eta_{p+2}$.
 \item If $\eta^E_{p+2}\ne0$ and $G_{p+2}(\varpi)\ne0$, then on the boundary rays the extrapolated candidate satisfies $M_{E_nR,\varpi}(x)=(-1)^{p+2}\eta^E_{p+2}G_{p+2}(\varpi)x^{p+2}+O(x^{p+3})$, so for small steps it is CPTP if and only if $(-1)^p\eta^E_{p+2}G_{p+2}(\varpi)>0$.
 \item If $R$ is the truncated exponential of degree $p$, which covers the forward Euler, every two-stage second-order, the SSPRK(3,3), and the classical fourth-order method, then
 $\eta^E_{p+2}=-\dfrac{(n-1)(p+1)}{n(n^p-1)(p+2)!}<0$; since $G_{p+2}(0)=1-2^{-(p+1)}>0$, at $\varpi=0$ the extrapolate is locally non-CPTP for even $p$ and locally CPTP for odd $p$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
$E_n$ fixes $\e^z$. Write $\delta(w)=\eta_{p+1}w^{p+1}+\eta_{p+2}w^{p+2}+O(w^{p+3})$, so that $R(z/n)^n=\big(\e^{z/n}+\delta(z/n)\big)^n=\e^z+n\,\e^{(n-1)z/n}\delta(z/n)+O(z^{2p+2})$ with $2p+2\ge p+3$. Expanding $\e^{(n-1)z/n}=1+(n-1)z/n+O(z^2)$,
\[
 n\,\e^{(n-1)z/n}\delta(z/n)=\frac{\eta_{p+1}}{n^p}z^{p+1}+\frac{(n-1)\eta_{p+1}+\eta_{p+2}}{n^{p+1}}z^{p+2}+O(z^{p+3}).
\]
Multiplying by $n^p$, subtracting $R(z)-\e^z$, and dividing by $n^p-1$ cancels the $z^{p+1}$ term and leaves \eqref{eq:extrapolation_defect}. Item~2 is Theorem~\ref{thm:defect} with $m=p+2$, since $E_nR$ has real coefficients. Item~3 follows from $\eta_{p+1}=-1/(p+1)!$, $\eta_{p+2}=-1/(p+2)!$, and $(-1)^{p+2}=(-1)^p$.
\end{proof}

For $p=4$ and $n=2$ the corollary gives $\eta^E_6=-1/4320$ and, with $G_6$ from \eqref{eq:G_factors}, an extrapolate of the classical method that is locally non-CPTP for $\varpi<\sqrt{2-\sqrt{33}/4}$, CPTP between the two zeros of $G_6$, and non-CPTP above $\sqrt{2+\sqrt{33}/4}$; the Dormand--Prince principal formula has the opposite sequence because it has the same $G_6$ but the opposite sign of $\eta_6$. Section~\ref{sec:candidates} upgrades the local statement at $\varpi=0$ to a global one. The exponent in $E_n$ is the linear order $p$, which coincides with the classical order for the methods used here; for a method whose linear order exceeds its classical order the standard Richardson weights use the classical order, and the same computation applies with that exponent. The case $\eta_{p+1}=\eta_{p+2}$ of item~1 occurs when $R(z)\e^{-z}-1$ has no $z^{p+2}$ term, as for the symmetric implicit midpoint rule, and then extrapolation gains two orders (Online Resource~1, Table~S2). As a consistency check, the two-fold extrapolate of the forward Euler method is the second-order polynomial $1+z+z^2/2$, whose admissible set on nonrotating one-way damping is $[0,2]$ (Online Resource~1, Section~9); the per-method values of $\eta^E_{p+2}$ for $n=2,3,4$ are tabulated in Online Resource~1.

\section{Exact classification for the classical fourth-order method}\label{sec:rk4}

On the boundary rays $\theta\in\{0,1\}$, $u=0$, $\nu=\pm\varpi x$ the classical fourth-order method, $R_4(z)=1+z+z^2/2+z^3/6+z^4/24$, admits a complete description of $\Adm_4(\varpi)$. Its population multiplier satisfies
\begin{equation}
 24R_4(-x)=(x^2-2x)^2+8(x^2-3x+3)>0\qquad(x\ge0),
 \label{eq:rk4_population}
\end{equation}
so $a\ge0$ never binds, while the ceiling $a\le1$ first binds at
\begin{equation}
 \alpha_4=2.785293563405\ldots,\qquad \alpha_4^3-4\alpha_4^2+12\alpha_4-24=0,
 \label{eq:alpha4}
\end{equation}
the unique positive root of that cubic. Set $q=1+4\varpi^2$ and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 q_\pm&=\frac{123\pm11\sqrt{33}}{16},\qquad
 \varpi_\pm=\frac{\sqrt{107\pm11\sqrt{33}}}{8},\\
 \varpi_-&=0.82736225145\ldots,\qquad \varpi_+=1.63071202389\ldots,
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:qcrit}
\end{equation}
so that $q=q_\pm$ exactly at $\varpi=\varpi_\pm$. The margin factors for every frequency.

\begin{proposition}[Margin factorization]\label{prop:rk4factor}
On the laboratory-frame boundary rays $\theta\in\{0,1\}$, $u=0$, $\nu=\pm\varpi x$, for the classical method $R_4$ and $q=1+4\varpi^2$,
\begin{equation}
 M_4(x;\varpi)=-\frac{x^5q}{147456}\,\mathcal C_q(qx),\qquad
 \mathcal C_q(y)=y^3-16y^2-32(q-6)y+384(q-4),
 \label{eq:rk4_factor}
\end{equation}
with discriminant $\operatorname{disc}_y\mathcal C_q=16384\,(q-4)\,(8q^2-123q+348)$, which vanishes exactly at $q=4$ and $q=q_\pm$. The cubic $q\mapsto\mathcal C_q(q\alpha_4)$, whose coefficients lie in $\mathbb Q(\alpha_4)$, has exactly one real root, $q_c=3.5282058982\ldots$ ($\varpi_c=0.7950166505\ldots$), and it lies in $(1,q_-)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Expanding $R_4(-x)-|R_4(-\alpha x)|^2$ with $\alpha=\tfrac12-\ii\varpi$ and collecting powers of $x$ gives \eqref{eq:rk4_factor}, and the discriminant is a direct computation. The discriminant of the cubic $q\mapsto\mathcal C_q(q\alpha_4)$, reduced modulo $\alpha_4^3-4\alpha_4^2+12\alpha_4-24$, equals $-1774190592\alpha_4^2+3472883712\alpha_4+3170893824<0$, the sign being decided on an isolating interval of $\alpha_4$, so the cubic has exactly one real root. Its resultant with the minimal polynomial of $\alpha_4$ is an integer polynomial of degree nine whose unique real root a Sturm sequence isolates in $(1,q_-)$, and the sign change of $\mathcal C_q(q\alpha_4)$ across that bracket identifies the root as $q_c$ (Online Resource~1, Section~3). All identities are reproduced symbolically in the code archive.
\end{proof}

The critical cubics factor as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \mathcal C_{q_-}(y)&=(y-9+\sqrt{33})^2(y-2\sqrt{33}+2),\\
 \mathcal C_4(y)&=y(y-8)^2,\\
 \mathcal C_{q_+}(y)&=(y-9-\sqrt{33})^2(y+2+2\sqrt{33}),
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:critical_cubics}
\end{equation}
direct computations that are reproduced symbolically in the code archive.

\begin{theorem}[Classification of $\Adm_4(\varpi)$]\label{thm:rk4}
On the rays of Proposition~\ref{prop:rk4factor} write $\Adm_4(\varpi)$ for the admissible set $\Adm_{R_4}(\varpi)$ of the direct candidate at $(\theta,\kappa)\in\{(0,0),(1,0)\}$, and let $y_1\le y_2\le y_3$ denote the real roots of $\mathcal C_q$ when there are three, and $y_3$ its real root when there is one. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item For $1\le q<q_-$: $\Adm_4=[0,\min\{\alpha_4,y_3/q\}]$; explicitly, $\Adm_4=[0,\alpha_4]$ for $q\le q_c$ and $\Adm_4=[0,y_3/q]$ with $y_3/q<\alpha_4$ for $q_c<q<q_-$. At $q=q_-$ the same formula holds and the double root $y=9-\sqrt{33}$ is an interior zero of the margin.
 \item For $q_-<q<4$: $\mathcal C_q$ has three positive roots and $\Adm_4=[0,y_1/q]\cup[y_2/q,y_3/q]$.
 \item At $q=4$ ($\varpi=\sqrt3/2$): $\Adm_4=\{0,2\}$.
 \item For $4<q<q_+$: $\Adm_4=\{0\}$.
 \item At $q=q_+$: $\Adm_4=\{0,(9+\sqrt{33})/q_+\}$.
 \item For $q>q_+$: $\mathcal C_q$ has one negative and two positive roots $y_2<y_3$, and $\Adm_4=\{0\}\cup[y_2/q,y_3/q]$.
\end{enumerate}
All endpoints in items 2, 5, and 6 lie below $\alpha_4$, and the isolated points in items 3 and 5 are exact members of $\Adm_4$ at which the margin vanishes.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
For $x>0$, \eqref{eq:rk4_factor} makes the block condition $M_4\ge0$ equivalent to $\mathcal C_q(qx)\le0$, and \eqref{eq:rk4_population} reduces the population conditions to $x\le\alpha_4$. The sign of $\mathcal C_q$ at $y=0$ is that of $384(q-4)$. By the discriminant, $\mathcal C_q$ has one real root for $q<q_-$ and for $4<q<q_+$, and three real roots (with multiplicity) for $q_-\le q\le4$ and $q\ge q_+$. By Vieta, the roots have sum $16$, pairwise sum $32(6-q)$, and product $-384(q-4)$. For $q\le4$ no root is negative, because for $y<0$ every term of $\mathcal C_q(y)=y^3-16y^2+32(6-q)y+384(q-4)$ is nonpositive and the first is negative; hence with three real roots ($q_-\le q\le4$) all are nonnegative (strictly positive for $q<4$), and with one real root ($q<q_-$) it is positive; for $q>4$ the product is negative, so the single real root ($4<q<q_+$) is negative, and with three real roots ($q\ge q_+$) exactly one is negative. Hence for $4<q<q_+$ the cubic is positive on $y>0$ and no positive step is admissible, which is item~4; for $q>q_+$ the margin is nonnegative exactly on $[y_2/q,y_3/q]$, for $q_-<q<4$ exactly on $[0,y_1/q]\cup[y_2/q,y_3/q]$, and for $q<q_-$ exactly on $[0,y_3/q]$. Intersecting with $x\le\alpha_4$ gives items~1, 2, and 6 once the position of $y_3/q$ relative to $\alpha_4$ is known. Put $g(q)=\mathcal C_q(q\alpha_4)$; by Proposition~\ref{prop:rk4factor}, $g<0$ on $[1,q_c)$ and $g>0$ on $(q_c,\infty)$. For $q<10/3$ the derivative $\mathcal C_q'$ has no real zero, its discriminant being $128(3q-10)$, so $\mathcal C_q$ is increasing and $\operatorname{sgn}g(q)=\operatorname{sgn}(q\alpha_4-y_3)$. For $q\ge10/3$ let $y^*=\big(16+\sqrt{96q-320}\big)/3$ be the larger critical point of $\mathcal C_q$; then $q\alpha_4>y^*$ for every such $q$, because $(3\alpha_4q-16)^2-(96q-320)$ is a quadratic in $q$ with positive leading coefficient and discriminant $-2304(\alpha_4-2)(5\alpha_4+2)<0$. Since $\mathcal C_q$ is increasing on $[y^*,\infty)$, $y_3\ge y^*$ whenever there are three real roots, and $\mathcal C_q>0$ on $(y_3,\infty)$ in every case, $g(q)>0$ implies $q\alpha_4>y_3$ and $g(q)<0$ implies $q\alpha_4<y_3$. Hence $y_3/q>\alpha_4$ on $[1,q_c)$, $y_3/q=\alpha_4$ at $q_c$, and $y_3/q<\alpha_4$ on $(q_c,\infty)\supset[q_-,4]\cup[q_+,\infty)$, which gives the crossover in item~1 and places every endpoint of items 2, 5, and 6 below $\alpha_4$; the sign charts of every regime are listed in Online Resource~1 (Section~3). The equality cases follow from \eqref{eq:critical_cubics}: at $q=4$, $\mathcal C_4\le0$ on $y\ge0$ only at $y\in\{0,8\}$, giving the isolated step $x=2$; at $q=q_+$ the cubic is nonnegative on $y\ge0$ with the single zero $y=9+\sqrt{33}$; at $q=q_-$ the double root lies inside $[0,y_3]$, so the margin touches zero at an interior point without leaving the set.
\end{proof}

Figure~\ref{fig:rk4} shows two forms of reentrance. Just below $\varpi=\sqrt3/2$ a detached interval coexists with the attached small-step interval. The band $\varpi_-<\varpi<\sqrt3/2$ is only $0.0386631\ldots$ wide and can be missed by a coarse frequency grid. At $\sqrt3/2$ both components collapse to points; positive-step admissibility then disappears until a detached interval emerges at $\varpi_+$. This is a saddle-node bifurcation: the two positive roots separate as $O\big((\varpi-\varpi_+)^{1/2}\big)$ (Online Resource~1, Fig.~S2a), making companion-matrix roots ill-conditioned nearby. We therefore establish the classification by exact Sturm isolation and sign evaluation, using floating-point roots only to initialize the plots. The isolated steps at $q=4$ and $q=q_+$ belong to the channel set but have zero margin, making them unsuitable as robust controller targets (Section~\ref{sec:guard}).

\section{Representation and candidate construction}\label{sec:candidates}

\subsection{Rotating frame}\label{sec:frame}

Let $\tilde\rho(t)=\e^{\ii Ht}\rho(t)\e^{-\ii Ht}$. Because $\e^{\ii Ht}\sigma_\pm\e^{-\ii Ht}=\e^{\pm\ii\omega t}\sigma_\pm$ and $\mathcal D[\e^{\ii\phi}L]=\mathcal D[L]$, the transformed generator $\Ldis$ is \eqref{eq:gksl} with $\omega=0$: the interaction picture removes the coherent precession exactly and leaves the dissipators unchanged. In superoperator form $\Lgen=\Lgen_H+\Ldis$ with $[\Lgen_H,\Ldis]=0$, so $\e^{h\Lgen}=\Uh_h\,\e^{h\Ldis}$, where $\Uh_h(Y)=\e^{-\ii Hh}Y\e^{\ii Hh}$ is the unitary channel of the back-rotation. The \emph{rotating-frame candidate} is the map obtained by applying the RK method to the transformed equation and rotating back exactly,
\begin{equation}
 \Phi^{\rm RF}_R(x)=\Uh_h\circ R(h\Ldis).
 \label{eq:rf_candidate}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Representation dependence]\label{prop:frame}
(i) $\Phi^{\rm RF}_R(x)$ is CPTP if and only if $R(h\Ldis)$ is CPTP; its admissible set is therefore $\Adm_R(0)$ for every $\omega$. (ii) Let $\omega\ne0$ and let $R$ be a polynomial of degree $d\ge1$, which covers every explicit method. Then the laboratory-frame candidate $R(h\Lgen)$ and the rotating-frame candidate \eqref{eq:rf_candidate} are different linear maps: their coherence multipliers have different moduli at all but finitely many steps of any bounded interval. The same holds for the backward Euler and implicit midpoint stability functions.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(i) For every linear map $\Phi$ on $M_2(\mathbb C)$ and every unitary channel $\Uh$, $\Phi$ is completely positive if and only if $\Uh\circ\Phi$ is: compositions of completely positive maps are completely positive, and $\Uh^{-1}(Y)=\e^{\ii Hh}Y\e^{-\ii Hh}$ is itself a unitary channel, so $\Phi=\Uh^{-1}\circ(\Uh\circ\Phi)$. Since $\Uh_h$ preserves the trace and the multipliers of $R(h\Ldis)$ are the real numbers $R(-x)$ and $R(-x/2-u)$, which do not involve $\omega$, (i) follows. (ii) The coherence multiplier of \eqref{eq:rf_candidate} is $\e^{\ii\nu}R(-x/2-u)$ and that of $R(h\Lgen)$ is $R(-x/2-u+\ii\nu)$. With $u=\kappa x$ and $\nu=\varpi x$ their squared moduli are polynomials in $x$ whose coefficients of $x^{2d}$ are $r_d^2(\tfrac12+\kappa)^{2d}$ and $r_d^2\big((\tfrac12+\kappa)^2+\varpi^2\big)^d$, where $r_d\ne0$ is the leading coefficient of $R$; for $\varpi\ne0$ these differ, so the two polynomials are distinct and agree at finitely many $x$. For $R(z)=1/(1-z)$ and $R(z)=(1+z/2)/(1-z/2)$ the differences of the squared moduli are $-\nu^2/\big((1+s)^2((1+s)^2+\nu^2)\big)$ and $8\nu^2s/\big((2+s)^2((2+s)^2+\nu^2)\big)$ with $s=x/2+u$, which vanish for $\nu\ne0$ only at $s=0$.
\end{proof}

For the classical fourth-order method on one-way, zero-dephasing damping the rotating-frame candidate is admissible exactly on $[0,\alpha_4]$ for every precession frequency, whereas the laboratory-frame candidate has the frequency-dependent sets of Theorem~\ref{thm:rk4}. The detached high-frequency components, empty regime, and isolated steps thus depend on the representation as well as the method. Complete positivity is invariant under unitary conjugation, but discretizing before or after the transformation gives different maps. A static frame removes only a commuting precession; it cannot remove the noncommuting perturbation of Section~\ref{sec:noncommuting}.

\subsection{Composition and substep ladders}\label{sec:composition}

\begin{proposition}[Composition and dilation]\label{prop:composition}
For two maps of the form \eqref{eq:direct_map} with the same $\theta$,
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_\theta(a_2,c_2)\circ\Phi_\theta(a_1,c_1)=\Phi_\theta(a_2a_1,\,c_2c_1),
 \label{eq:composition}
\end{equation}
so the equal-substep candidate has multipliers $a_n=R(-x/n)^n$ and $c_n=R\big(-(x/2+u-\ii\nu)/n\big)^n$. On the boundary rays $\theta\in\{0,1\}$, $u=0$, if the substep population multiplier $a_0=R(-x/n)$ is nonnegative, then $\Phi_R^{(n)}(x)$ is CPTP if and only if $\Phi_R(x/n)$ is. For the classical fourth-order method, whose population multiplier is positive by \eqref{eq:rk4_population}, $\Adm_4^{(n)}(\varpi)=n\,\Adm_4(\varpi)$ for every $n\ge1$ and every $\varpi$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Substituting \eqref{eq:direct_map} into itself shows that the population deviation from the common fixed point $\theta$ is multiplied by $a_2a_1$ and the coherence by $c_2c_1$. If $a_0\ge0$ then $a_0^n\le1$ exactly when $a_0\le1$, and $a_0^n-|c_0|^{2n}=(a_0-|c_0|^2)\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}a_0^{n-1-j}|c_0|^{2j}$ has the sign of $a_0-|c_0|^2$ whenever the sum is positive, which fails only in the trivial case $a_0=c_0=0$.
\end{proof}

Composition therefore neither repairs a failed substep nor spoils an admissible one on these rays (the one-way form of the first statement was observed in \cite{PierpointBernardLiu2026Note}): substepping dilates the admissible set without changing its shape. This has a sharp consequence for step doubling, which builds a coarse full-step candidate and a fine two-substep candidate over the same interval \cite{Shampine1985Doubling}.

\begin{proposition}[Substep ladder]\label{prop:ladder}
Suppose $\Adm_R$ has a positive-step component $\mathcal I=[x_-,x_+]$ with $0<x_-<x_+$ and that $\Adm_R^{(n)}=n\Adm_R$. Then the corresponding components $\mathcal I^{(n)}=n\mathcal I$ and $\mathcal I^{(n+1)}$ intersect if and only if $x_+/x_-\ge(n+1)/n$. In particular the full-step and two-half-step components are disjoint if and only if $x_+/x_-<2$, which holds if and only if there are initial proposals from which repeated halving skips $\mathcal I$; a fixed reduction factor $\rho\in(0,1)$ reaches $\mathcal I$ from every proposal above it if and only if $\rho\ge x_-/x_+$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$\mathcal I^{(n)}=[nx_-,nx_+]$ and $\mathcal I^{(n+1)}=[(n+1)x_-,(n+1)x_+]$ intersect exactly when $(n+1)x_-\le nx_+$. Two consecutive proposals $\rho^jx_0>x_+$ and $\rho^{j+1}x_0<x_-$ exist exactly when $\rho<x_-/x_+$.
\end{proof}

\begin{example}[Disjoint step-doubling candidates at $\varpi=2$]\label{ex:varpi2}
Exact isolation gives
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \Adm_4(2)&=\{0\}\cup[0.7447824278959\ldots,\,1.2703346269738\ldots],\\
 \Adm_4^{(2)}(2)&=\{0\}\cup[1.4895648557918\ldots,\,2.5406692539477\ldots],
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:varpi2_sets}
\end{equation}
whose positive-step components are disjoint because $x_+/x_-=1.7056\ldots<2$: at total step $x=1$ the full-step candidate is a channel and the two-half-step candidate is not, and at $x=2$ the statuses reverse (Fig.~\ref{fig:candidates}a). The pair $n=1,2$ is the most restrictive rung of the ladder; at this frequency $\mathcal I^{(n)}$ and $\mathcal I^{(n+1)}$ intersect for every $n\ge2$. Disjointness and the halving-skip condition end together at the frequency $\varpi_{1/2}=2.248254604514980\ldots$ at which $x_+/x_-=2$; the condition $y_3=2y_2$ on the roots of $\mathcal C_q$ has resultant $6291456\,(q-4)\,(72q^3-2071q^2+12552q-21744)$, and $\varpi_{1/2}$ corresponds to the unique root of the cubic factor above $q_+$ (its two smaller roots lie in the reentrant band and do not bound a detached component).
\end{example}

Many step-doubling codes advance the fine candidate and use the coarse one only to estimate the error. Only the advanced candidate needs a channel certificate, but that certificate cannot be transferred from the other candidate. Requiring both candidates to be channels leaves no admissible positive step at $\varpi=2$.

\subsection{Richardson extrapolation}\label{sec:richardson}

The set of CPTP maps is convex, so a convex combination of channels is a channel; trace preservation survives every affine combination whose weights sum to one, but a negative weight can leave the channel set \cite{Choi1975CP}. For the classical fourth-order method let $\Phi_{\rm c}(x)=\Phi_4(x)$ and $\Phi_{\rm f}(x)=\Phi_4(x/2)^2$ be the coarse and fine candidates and
\begin{equation}
 \Phi_{\rm ext}(x)=\frac{16\,\Phi_{\rm f}(x)-\Phi_{\rm c}(x)}{15}
 \label{eq:richardson_map}
\end{equation}
the standard Richardson extrapolate ($n=2$, $p=4$ in Definition~\ref{def:candidate}). By Proposition~\ref{prop:composition} and the affine structure of \eqref{eq:direct_map}, $\Phi_{\rm ext}$ is the phase-covariant map generated by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 R_{\rm ext}(z)&=\frac{16R_4(z/2)^2-R_4(z)}{15}=\sum_{j=0}^{5}\frac{z^j}{j!}+\frac{z^6}{864}+\frac{z^7}{8640}+\frac{z^8}{138240},\\
 R_{\rm ext}(z)-\e^z&=-\frac{z^6}{4320}+O(z^7),
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:richardson_stability}
\end{equation}
in agreement with Corollary~\ref{cor:extrapolation}. The corollary's local statement at $\varpi=0$ becomes global.

\begin{theorem}[Loss of complete positivity under extrapolation]\label{thm:richardson}
On nonrotating one-way damping ($\varpi=0$, $u=0$, $\theta\in\{0,1\}$), $\Phi_{\rm c}(x)$ is CPTP for $0\le x\le\alpha_4$ and $\Phi_{\rm f}(x)$ for $0\le x\le2\alpha_4$, whereas $\Phi_{\rm ext}(x)$ is not CPTP for any $0<x\le\alpha_4$. Its margin factors as
\begin{equation}
 M_{\rm ext}(x)=-\frac{x^6\,P_{10}(x)}{1252412463513600}=-\frac{31}{138240}\,x^6+O(x^7),
 \label{eq:richardson_margin}
\end{equation}
where $P_{10}$ is the degree-ten integer polynomial listed in Online Resource~1, whose smallest positive root $6.034523958649\ldots$ exceeds $\alpha_4$. The extrapolated candidate re-enters the channel set only on the remote interval $[6.034523958649\ldots,\,6.459127767825\ldots]$, whose upper end is the population-ceiling exit $R_{\rm ext}(-x)=1$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
$\Adm_4(0)=[0,\alpha_4]$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:rk4} and $\Adm_4^{(2)}(0)=[0,2\alpha_4]$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:composition}. Corollary~\ref{cor:extrapolation} with $p=4$, $n=2$ gives the leading coefficient $-\tfrac1{4320}G_6(0)=-\tfrac{31}{138240}$; exact substitution into $R_{\rm ext}(-x)-R_{\rm ext}(-x/2)^2$ produces the factor $P_{10}$, and a Sturm sequence places its first positive zero above $\alpha_4$, so the margin is negative on $(0,\alpha_4]$. The complete sign chart of the margin, of $R_{\rm ext}(-x)$, and of $1-R_{\rm ext}(-x)$ in Online Resource~1 isolates the remote component.
\end{proof}

Codes that advance $\Phi_{\rm f}$ and use the extrapolate only as an error indicator are unaffected by this loss of complete positivity. Advancing the extrapolate, however, requires testing its own channel condition, regardless of its order or the physicality of its constituents. At $\omega=0$ the laboratory-frame and rotating-frame constructions coincide, so the failure occurs for both. The frequency-dependent orientation bands of Section~\ref{sec:extrapolation} concern the laboratory-frame candidate. Figure~\ref{fig:candidates}b shows the scaled extrapolation margin, which is negative on $(0,\alpha_4]$.

\begin{remark}[Convergence from outside the channel set]\label{rem:fixed_horizon}
Candidate dependence does not conflict with convergence. On nonrotating one-way damping take $N$ equal steps $x_N=X/N$ to a fixed horizon $X$ and let $R(z)-\e^z=\eta_mz^m+O(z^{m+1})$. Then the margin of the composed map satisfies
$M_N(X)=K_m\,X\e^{-X}(X/N)^{m-1}+O(N^{-m})$ with $K_m=(-1)^m(1-2^{1-m})\eta_m$ (Online Resource~1, Section~8). When $K_m<0$ the composed candidate converges to the exact channel while remaining outside the channel set at every sufficiently fine resolution, with a negative Choi eigenvalue that decays at the global order $m-1$; forward Euler and SSPRK(3,3) are examples with exact negative margins throughout their positive-population range. Convergence of the maps therefore says nothing about the physicality of any finite candidate, as noted in \cite{PierpointBernardLiu2026Note}.
\end{remark}

\section{Robustness and certified adaptive control}\label{sec:guard}

We next examine persistence of the disconnected structure under the buffers in \eqref{eq:buffers} and a noncommuting perturbation, then use the criterion to certify steps in an adaptive method. The acceptance test rejects unresolved cases.

\subsection{Persistence under buffers}\label{sec:persistence}

\begin{theorem}[Relative-open persistence of disconnectedness]\label{thm:persistence}
Fix a method of order at least two, a ratio $\varpi$, and a compact step interval $\mathcal K$ contained in the stage-evaluation domain. Suppose that at $(\theta,\kappa)=(0,0)$: (i) the margin has a detached positive component $(r_2,r_3)\subset\mathcal K$ bounded by simple roots; (ii) there are points $s_-\in(0,r_2)$, $s_+\in(r_2,r_3)$, and $s_{\rm out}>r_3$ in $\mathcal K$ at which the margin has the signs $-,+,-$; (iii) every inactive constraint in \eqref{eq:criterion} has uniform strict slack on a closed neighbourhood of the entire interval $[r_2,r_3]$ and on closed neighbourhoods of the exterior sample points, all contained in $\mathcal K$. Then there is a relative-open neighbourhood $N$ of $(0,0)$ in the quadrant $\theta,\kappa\ge0$ such that for every $(\theta,\kappa)\in N$ with $\theta+\kappa>0$ the admissible set has at least two positive-step components, an attached interval and a detached interval near $[r_2,r_3]$. Nothing is asserted about the steps between the attached interval and $s_-$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The three strict signs, the uniform slack of the inactive constraints, and (for implicit methods) the open stage-evaluation condition persist on a relative-open parameter neighbourhood by continuity and compactness; the implicit function theorem continues the two simple roots, the buffers \eqref{eq:buffers} supply the attached interval, and the persistent positivity of the margin on $[r_2+\varepsilon,r_3-\varepsilon]$ gives the detached component. The details are in Online Resource~1, Section~4.
\end{proof}

For the classical fourth-order method at $\varpi=2$ the hypotheses hold with $s_-=1/2$, $s_+=1$, $s_{\rm out}=3/2$, and Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}a shows exact certified components $[0,r_1]\cup[r_2,r_3]$ for five buffered cases (twelve-digit endpoints in Online Resource~1, Section~4): every endpoint is a simple root isolated by a Sturm count, every intervening sign is evaluated at an exact rational sample, and the attached interval grows with the buffer as \eqref{eq:buffers} predicts, from $r_1=0.1222\ldots$ at $(\theta,\kappa)=(10^{-3},0)$ to $r_1=0.5193\ldots$ at $(0,10^{-2})$. A multiplicative step reducer with factor $\rho$ started above the attached endpoint $x_{\rm att}$ needs about $\lceil\log(x_{\rm att}/x_0)/\log\rho\rceil$ reductions; the joint-limit estimates $x_{\rm att}\sim(48\kappa)^{1/4}/\varpi$ and $x_{\rm att}\sim(24\theta(1-\theta)/\varpi^4)^{1/3}$, from \eqref{eq:buffers} against the large-frequency defect $-\varpi^4x^5/24$, show that recovery by refinement alone is expensive when the buffer is weak.

\subsection{A noncommuting perturbation}\label{sec:noncommuting}

The two-mode reduction is special to phase covariance, but disconnectedness is not. Consider
\begin{equation}
 \Lgen_{\Omega_x}(\rho)=\Lgen(\rho)-\ii\big[\Omega_x\sigma_x/2,\rho\big],
 \label{eq:transverse}
\end{equation}
with the dissipators of \eqref{eq:gksl}. For $\Omega_x\ne0$ phase covariance is broken, no static frame removes the precession, and the Choi matrix of $R_4(h\Lgen_{\Omega_x})$ must be assembled from the full $4\times4$ superoperator (Online Resource~1, Section~4).

\begin{lemma}[Persistence in a parameterized family]\label{prop:noncommuting}
Let $\Lgen_\eta$ be a continuous finite-dimensional family of GKSL generators. If four steps $0<h_a<h_g<h_d<h_o$ give strict signs $+,-,+,-$ of the smallest Choi eigenvalue of $R_4(h\Lgen_{\eta_0})$, then the same sign pattern, and hence at least two positive-step completely positive components, persists for all $\eta$ in a neighbourhood of $\eta_0$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The Choi matrix of $R_4(h\Lgen_\eta)$ is Hermitian and depends continuously on $(h,\eta)$, and the smallest eigenvalue of a Hermitian matrix is continuous, so four strict signs remain unchanged on a common neighbourhood; two positive samples separated by a negative one cannot lie in one component.
\end{proof}

At $\Gamma=1$, $\theta=\kappa=10^{-3}$, $\omega/\Gamma=2$, and $\Omega_x/\Gamma=0.1$, exact characteristic-polynomial brackets give the smallest Choi eigenvalues
$9.5156\times10^{-5}$, $-2.7778\times10^{-3}$, $5.8959\times10^{-4}$, and $-3.9227\times10^{-1}$ at $h=0.1$, $0.5$, $0.9$, and $1.4$, and exact isolation of the roots of every principal minor yields
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \{h\in[0,1.5]:\ R_4(h\Lgen_{0.1})\ \text{is CPTP}\}
 ={}&[0,\,0.26357690\ldots]\\
 &\cup[0.73588381\ldots,\,1.26847247\ldots].
\end{aligned}
 \label{eq:noncommuting_set}
\end{equation}
Repeating the exact computation at $\Omega_x/\Gamma\in\{0,0.05,0.10,0.20\}$ gives an attached and a detached component in each case (Fig.~\ref{fig:robustness}b; endpoints in Online Resource~1). These four pointwise certificates leave intermediate values of $\Omega_x$ uncertified; Lemma~\ref{prop:noncommuting} establishes local persistence around each certified point.

\subsection{Certified acceptance of the executed candidate}\label{sec:certified}

Adaptive step-size control proposes steps from local error estimates \cite{DormandPrince1980,HairerNorsettWanner1993,Shampine1985Doubling,Soderlind2002}. We supplement the error test with a complete-positivity certificate for the proposed update. A failed certificate rejects that update without introducing an artificial error ratio into the controller's history.

\paragraph{What is certified.} The certificate concerns the exact linear map $\Phi_{\rm spec}(x)$ determined by the candidate construction rule of Definition~\ref{def:candidate} and by the exact rational value of the binary64 step that the code executes (never a printed decimal). The outcomes are
\begin{equation}
 \PASS,\qquad \FAIL,\qquad \UNCERTAIN,
 \label{eq:tri_state}
\end{equation}
where \PASS\ and \FAIL\ are exact decisions of \eqref{eq:criterion} and of the stage-domain predicates by rational arithmetic and, when roots must be located, by Sturm isolation, and \UNCERTAIN\ is returned whenever a sign, a root identity, an ordering, or a root separation cannot be decided at the working precision; it is never converted into acceptance. The complex128 matrix multiplied into the accumulated propagator is a rounding of $\Phi_{\rm spec}(x)$, and no error enclosure is claimed for the floating-point products that follow.

On the one-way ray the margin of the classical method behaves as $-x^5q\,\mathcal C_q(qx)/147456$ by \eqref{eq:rk4_factor}. Direct floating-point evaluation of $R_4(-x)-|R_4(-\alpha x)|^2$ therefore loses the sign to cancellation as $x\to0$ (Online Resource~1, Fig.~S2c), and a tolerance-based eigenvalue test accepts non-channels at small steps. Near an endpoint, decimal-to-binary conversion can also change the sign: at $\varpi=2$ the decimal $x=1.270334626973889$ has margin $+1.43\times10^{-16}$, whereas the binary64 number displayed by those digits has margin $-9.45\times10^{-17}$.

\paragraph{Projection.} Let $\Adm^{\rm cand}(x_{\rm prop})$ be the admissible set of the proposed candidate construction on $(0,x_{\rm prop}]$. For the polynomial candidates of Definition~\ref{def:candidate} on a phase-covariant ray the constraint functions are polynomials with rational coefficients, and a certified component locator returns the components of $\Adm^{\rm cand}(x_{\rm prop})$ by exact root isolation, deciding the membership of every root in the requested domain before restricting any bracket (Online Resource~1, Section~7). Every positive-width component contains an interior rational step, but a finite search over representable numbers need not reach a narrow component; the implementation therefore accepts a projection only if it finds a binary64 number whose exact rational value lies in the component and recertifies the candidate at that number. If the search or the recertification fails the outcome is \UNCERTAIN, and if no positive-width component exists the outcome is ``no component''. Feasibility says nothing about local accuracy, which is recomputed for the executed candidate.

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{Candidate-aware step with certified acceptance}\label{alg:guard}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require generator ray, RK method, candidate construction rule, error-controller proposal $x_{\rm prop}$, tolerance, physical fallback propagator
\State Construct the candidate intended for advancement at $x_{\rm prop}$ and its local-error estimate
\If{the error estimate exceeds the tolerance} update the controller proposal from the error and repeat \EndIf
\State Certify the candidate at the exact binary64 step; keep the error observation separate from the certificate
\If{\PASS} accept, and update the controller memory with this candidate's error
\ElsIf{\FAIL} locate the certified components of $\Adm^{\rm cand}(x_{\rm prop})$; if a positive-width component exists, project to a certified interior binary64 step, recompute the error there, and return to line~2; otherwise switch representation or propagator (fallback), construct the replacement candidate, and return to line~2
\ElsIf{\UNCERTAIN} increase the working precision or construct the fallback and return to line~2; never convert \UNCERTAIN\ into acceptance
\EndIf
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:guard} keeps error control and complete-positivity control separate: a rejection caused only by complete positivity leaves the controller's error memory unchanged, the error is recomputed at any projected candidate before acceptance, and a change of representation or propagator resets the memory because the preceding error ratio belongs to a different candidate family. At an exact one-way boundary with a detached component (Theorem~\ref{thm:rk4}, items 4--6) a proposal below the component has no smaller admissible direct step, so the algorithm must switch; with any buffer the attached interval of Theorem~\ref{thm:persistence} returns and projection becomes a cost decision. Exact-exponential and exact-subflow fallbacks receive a by-construction certificate only when built from a recognized construction rule with a nonnegative time argument, together with residual checks of the realized matrix; a caller-supplied matrix cannot be certified by a label.

\subsection{Adaptive tests}\label{sec:adaptive_tests}

The tests below probe fail-closed behaviour, not efficiency. Step doubling estimates the error from the coarse and fine candidates of the classical fourth-order method and advances the fine candidate; the error measure is the normalized Choi trace distance $\tfrac1{15}\cdot\tfrac12\big\|\big(J[\Phi_{\rm c}]-J[\Phi_{\rm f}]\big)/2\big\|_1$, the proposal rule is a proportional--integral controller with safety factor $0.9$, and the exact map $\e^{X\Lgen}$ over the whole horizon is the reference for the global error. Four policies are compared on the boundary case $\theta=\kappa=0$, $\varpi=2$, horizon $X=2$, initial step $1.4$: error-only laboratory-frame stepping, Algorithm~\ref{alg:guard} with the rotating-frame candidate as fallback, rotating-frame stepping throughout, and Strang splitting~\cite{Strang1968} of the exact coherent and dissipative subflows, which is exact for this commuting generator and is included as a roundoff-floor reference. Buffered ($\theta=0$, $\kappa=10^{-3}$, $\varpi=2$, initial step $0.3$) and near-saddle ($\theta=0$, $\kappa=10^{-3}$, $\varpi=1.65$, initial step $1.0$, horizon $X=1$) cases exercise direct retention and projection; ray parameters are entered as binary64 numbers and each certificate refers to their exact rational values. Table~\ref{tab:adaptive} reports accuracy and physicality separately; the accuracy--work relation over the tolerance sweep and the accepted candidate types are plotted in Online Resource~1, Fig.~S4.

\begin{remark}[Scope of the adaptive certificates]\label{rem:scope}
Each accepted step is a certified linear trace-preserving map, but when the step sequence depends on one particular input state the adaptive input--output procedure is nonlinear in that state and is not itself a reusable channel; that requires a state-independent error criterion. The certificates decide exact candidate specifications and do not bound the accumulated floating-point product.
\end{remark}

\section{Discussion and limitations}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Relation to classical stability notions.} Complete positivity imposes a coupled condition beyond absolute stability of the individual modes. On one-way damping both scalar multipliers can be contractive while $|c|^2\le a$ fails. The admissible set can also be disconnected (Theorem~\ref{thm:rk4}), whereas threshold-type positivity and contractivity results \cite{BolleyCrouzeix1978,Kraaijevanger1991Contractivity,Horvath2005Threshold,GottliebShuTadmor2001} describe the maximal attached interval. On the rays of Theorem~\ref{thm:rk4}, the per-mode absolute-stability sets are intervals; the coupled inequality produces the disconnection. Adaptive positivity by weight perturbation \cite{NussleinRanochaKetcheson2021} and the positivity barrier for exponential RK methods \cite{OstermannVanDaele2007} concern constraints on the solution vector of a general ODE. Here the constraint is on the map under tensor extension, with the RK formula unchanged.

\paragraph{Relation to structure-preserving propagators.} Kraus-form, integrating-factor, nested-Picard, Gregory, tensor-train, exponential Euler, and quantum-channel discretizations \cite{RieschPiklJirauschek2020,CaoLu2025,AppeloCheng2025,HuAppeloCheng2025NPI,HuAppeloCheng2026Gregory,DelMastroAppeloCheng2026TT,ChenEtAl2026,RobinRouchonSellem2025} build structural preservation into a modified update. Their guarantees differ: state-dependent trace normalization preserves the set of density matrices but is generally nonlinear, whereas a quantum channel is a linear CPTP map \cite{RobinRouchonSellem2025}. Gregory nested-Picard stability analysis \cite{HuAppeloCheng2026Gregory} studies the amplification matrix without that normalization, including the general-linear-method history for higher orders. Our criterion instead tests whether an RK update, formed directly or by substepping, extrapolation, or representation change, is a channel. It determines when a direct step is admissible, when a smaller certified step exists, and when a different representation or propagator is needed. Methods that preserve complete positivity by construction are alternatives when their order and cost are suitable; the present results explain failures that local error control alone does not detect.

\paragraph{Cost.} One membership decision costs two scalar evaluations of the stability function and four inequalities, plus the stage-domain check for implicit methods; locating every component or proving emptiness is real-root isolation on polynomials of moderate degree \cite{BasuPollackRoy2006}. The two-mode reduction does not extend at that cost to a black-box or higher-dimensional Liouvillian, where a map-level Choi test is substantially larger, as the transverse-field certificates of Section~\ref{sec:noncommuting} show already for one qubit.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The analysis concerns the autonomous phase-covariant qubit family. The six-regime classification applies to the classical fourth-order method on the laboratory-frame one-way, zero-dephasing ray; other methods have different admissible sets (Table~\ref{tab:methods}) and reversal frequencies. The persistence theorem is qualitative, the buffered sets are certified at the listed parameters, and the transverse-field evidence consists of four pointwise certificates. The disjointness at $\varpi=2$ concerns one full step versus two half steps, and Theorem~\ref{thm:richardson} concerns advancement of the extrapolated map. Certification applies to the specified linear map at exact binary64 inputs, rather than to the accumulated floating-point product or the nonlinear input--output map of a state-dependent adaptive procedure. A channel candidate may also have a larger local error than a non-channel one; the adaptive tests therefore report accuracy and physicality separately.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

For the autonomous phase-covariant qubit generator, two values of the Runge--Kutta stability function determine complete positivity through the known channel conditions. On the one-way, zero-dephasing rays, the first defect from the exponential gives the leading margin coefficient for direct steps and Richardson extrapolates; its sign determines local orientation when the coefficient is nonzero. For the classical fourth-order method on these rays, the complete classification contains a reentrant band, a regime without positive admissible steps, isolated admissible steps, and a detached high-frequency interval. Its critical frequencies are certified, and the detached interval has edges $3/\varpi^2$ and $2\sqrt2/\varpi$ to leading order.

For numerical integration, the relevant object is the update map in its chosen representation. Transforming to the rotating frame before discretization gives the zero-precession admissible set. One full step and two half steps can have disjoint positive-step admissible sets. On nonrotating one-way damping without dephasing, the fifth-order Richardson extrapolate of the classical method is not a channel at any positive step of the interval on which both constituents are channels. An adaptive method that requires complete positivity must therefore certify the map it advances at the executed step and reject unresolved cases. The exact acceptance test for phase-covariant candidates provides this check; in the boundary tests it rejects every non-channel update accepted by error control alone.
